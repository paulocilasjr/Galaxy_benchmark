## 1. Article, Analysis and Resource formats

Source: https://www.nature.com/nmeth/content

The page gives the same format for all three types, apart from the type names.

| Requirement | Article | Analysis | Resource |
|---|---|---|---|
| Definition | "a report describing a novel method or tool" | "a report presenting comprehensive performance comparisons of established, related methods or tools, of key importance to a field of research" | "a report describing a collection of tools or a large dataset of broad utility, interest and significance to a field of research" |
| Abstract | "up to 150 words, unreferenced." | same | same |
| Main text | "3,000 words (up to 5,000 words with editorial discretion), excluding abstract, Methods, references and figure legends." | same | same (page writes "5000") |
| Display items | "up to 6 figures and/or tables." | same | same |
| Section structure | "Introduction (without heading) / Results / Discussion / Online Methods." | same | same |
| Subheadings | "Results and Methods should be divided by topical subheadings; the Discussion does not contain subheadings." | same | same |
| References | "as a guideline, we typically recommend up to 50." | same | same |
| Other | Received/accepted dates; "may be accompanied by supplementary information"; peer reviewed | same | same |

What this means:

- **Introduction:** no heading.
- **Discussion:** no subheadings.
- **Results and Methods:** topical subheadings.
- **Methods placement:** an "Online Methods" section after the Discussion.
- **Reference limit:** about 50 for main-text references. The Nature Methods content page does not say whether Methods-only references count toward the 50. Nature's own guide says they do not: "Additional references for Methods or Supplementary Information are not included in this count." (https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/formatting-guide). That is a Nature rule, not a stated Nature Methods rule.

For comparison, the Brief Communication format from the same page: "Abstract – up to 70 words, unreferenced." "Main text – 1,200 words (up to 1600 words with editorial discretion), including abstract, references and figure legends." "The main text should not contain sections nor subheadings." "Display items – maximum 2 figures and/or tables (up to 3 items with editorial discretion)." "References – as a guideline, we typically recommend up to 20."

Note: the AIP page says figure legends must not exceed the article type's word limit ("Ensure the legend does not exceed the word limit of the article type."), but the content-types page gives no legend limit for Article, Analysis or Resource. The Nature flagship guide says "Legends should be fewer than 300 words each." That is a Nature rule, not a stated Nature Methods rule.

### Extended Data limits

The content-types page does not cover Extended Data. Two other Nature Methods pages do:

- AIP page: "A maximum of 10 Extended Data display figures is permitted." Also: "Figures can be multi-paneled, if necessary, and should be sized such that they will fit on a single PDF page." "Legends for Extended Data figures should be prepared as for main figures and should be included in the 'Inventory of Supporting Information' document." "All Extended Data figures must be referred to as discrete items at an appropriate place in the main text." "Extended Data figures are not copy-edited or styled by Nature Methods; for this reason, please follow journal style as closely as possible when preparing these figures."
- Preparing-your-material page: "Extended Data figures and tables are online-only display items that provide essential background to the main paper but are not included as main display items due to space constraints or being of interest only to specialists. A maximum of ten Extended Data display items is permitted."
- Note the wording differs: one page says "display figures", the other "display items" (figures and tables). The safe reading is no more than 10 Extended Data items in total.

### Supplementary Information (AIP page)

- "Each item should be designated as either Supplementary Equation, Discussion, Notes, Figure, Table, Video, Audio, Data or Software, and all except Notes should be numbered sequentially. This numbering should be separate from that used for items appearing either in the main article or in the Extended Data."
- "Supplementary Figures should be used only for cases when the use of Extended Data to report these findings is not appropriate. Each Supplementary Figure should fit, along with its legend, on a single PDF page."
- "Please submit supplementary text, figures, simple tables or data, and associated legends within a single combined PDF file."

### Other requirements relevant to these manuscripts

From the preparing-your-material page:

- On LLM use: "Large Language Models (LLMs), such as ChatGPT, do not currently satisfy our authorship criteria ... Use of an LLM should be properly documented in the Methods section (and if a Methods section is not available, in a suitable alternative part) of the manuscript."
- On Methods: "The Methods section should be written as concisely as possible but should contain all elements necessary to allow interpretation and replication of the results. The Methods sections of all original research papers will appear in all online versions."

From the initial-formatting page:

- "Your initial submission does not need to be specially formatted, as long as the study is described in a way that is suitable for editorial assessment and peer review." PDF, Word or compiled TeX are accepted.

## 2. Figures

### AIP page (https://www.nature.com/nmeth/submission-guidelines/aip-and-formatting, section 3)

- "All figures must be cited in sequence within the main article text in the form Fig. 1, Fig. 2."
- **Width and resolution:** "Figure panels should be prepared at a minimum resolution of 300 dpi and saved at a maximum width of 180 mm."
- **Font:** "Use a 5–7 pt san serif font for standard text labelling and Symbol font for Greek characters."
- "Use scale bars, not magnification factors, and include error bars where appropriate."
- "Do not flatten labelling or scale/error bars onto images".
- Figure legends: "Include a brief title for each figure with a short description of each panel cited in sequence." "Ensure the legend does not exceed the word limit of the article type." "Avoid methodological detail." "Use verbal cues to describe keys, eg. "open red triangles", not visual cues or symbols." "Include a description of centre values (median or average) and all error bars and how they were calculated. Give an indication of sample size (n number), state the statistical test used and provide P values."

### Final artwork guide (https://www.nature.com/documents/NRJs-guide-to-preparing-final-artwork.pdf)

This PDF is the "image preparation guidelines" linked from the AIP page.

- **Column widths** for "Original research and review content ... Includes: Analysis, Article, Brief Communication, ... Resource ...": "1-column width: 88 mm" and "2-column width: 180 mm".
- **Maximum figure height by caption length:**

| Caption length | 1-column (88 mm) | 2-column (180 mm) |
|---|---|---|
| <300 words | ~130 mm | ~185 mm |
| <150 words | ~180 mm | ~210 mm |
| <50 words | ~220 mm | ~225 mm |

- **Text:** "All text should be sans-serif typeface, preferably Helvetica or Arial." "Maximum text size is 7pt. Minimum text size is 5pt."
- **Colour:** "Original research content should be supplied in RGB colour mode."
- **File formats:** line art, graphs, charts and schematics should be vector files ("AI, EPS or PDF"); photos and complex illustrations should be bitmaps.

For comparison, the Nature flagship guide uses slightly different sizes: "Nature's standard figure sizes are 90 mm (single column) and 180 mm (double column) and the full depth of the page is 170 mm." For Nature Methods, use 88 mm / 180 mm from the guide above. The 180 mm maximum width is the same in both.

## 3. References

### Numbering order (AIP page, section 5)

"References should be numbered sequentially in the following order:

- Main text
- Tables
- Figure legends
- Box
- Methods
- Data Availability Section
- Extended data"

"References that are uncited or cited out of sequence may require manual reworking in production that could delay the production process."

### Methods references (AIP page, section 6)

"Methods should be subdivided by short, bold headings referring to methods used. ... If additional references are included, numbering should continue from the end of the last reference in the main paper (see section 5 References). Any Methods-only references should accompany the additional Methods at the end of the paper."

Methods-only references therefore continue the main-text numbering and are listed separately at the end of the Methods.

The Nature flagship guide states the same rule and adds one for Extended Data: "If further references are included in the Extended Data tables and Extended Data figure legends, the numbering should continue from the end of the last reference number in the main paper (or from the last reference number in the additional Methods section if present) and the list should be added to the end of the list accompanying the additional Methods section".

### Reference content rules (AIP page, section 5)

- "Only one publication is given for each reference."
- "Research objects (such as conference abstracts, numbered patents, datasets, protocols and code) that have been assigned a DOI are included in the reference list."
- "Unpublished meeting abstracts, papers in preparation and papers under review or in press without an available preprint should not appear in the reference list."
- "URLs for web sites should be cited parenthetically in the main text, not in the reference list." This applies to leaderboard pages, Hugging Face spaces, GitHub repositories without a DOI, and Galaxy server URLs.
- "Grant details and acknowledgments are not permitted as numbered references." "Footnotes are not supported."
- "For long-form manuscripts such as Articles, ... titles of cited articles are required." Example: "Eigler, D. M. & Schweizer, E. K. Positioning single atoms with a scanning tunnelling microscope. Nature 344, 524–526 (1990)."
- Data and code: "should include authors, title, publisher (repository name) and DOI expressed as a URL." Example: "Hao, Z., AghaKouchak, A., Nakhjiri, N. & Farahmand, A. Global Integrated Drought Monitoring and Prediction System (GIDMaPS) data sets. figshare http://dx.doi.org/10.6084/m9.figshare.853801 (2014)."
- Preprints. Example: "Babichev, S. A., Ries, J. & Lvovsky, A. I. Quantum scissors: teleportation of single-mode optical states by means of a nonlocal single photon. Preprint at https://arXiv.org/quant-ph/0208066 (2002)."
- LaTeX: "Please use numerical references only for citations, and include the references within the manuscript file itself." With BibTeX, paste the .bbl contents into the .tex file.

### Author count and typography: not stated by Nature Methods

The Nature Methods AIP page does not say when to use "et al.". These rules come from the Nature flagship guide (https://www.nature.com/nature/for-authors/formatting-guide). Every Nature Methods reference checked in this task follows them, as does the AIP example.

- "All authors should be included in reference lists unless there are more than five, in which case only the first author should be given, followed by 'et al.'." So five or fewer authors are listed in full, and six or more become "First, A. B. et al.".
- "Titles of articles cited in reference lists should be in upright, not italic text; the first word of the title is capitalized, the title written exactly as it appears in the work cited, ending with a full stop. Book titles are italic with all main words capitalized. Journal titles are italic and abbreviated according to common usage. Volume numbers are bold."
- "When cited in the text, reference numbers are superscript, not in brackets unless they are likely to be confused with a superscript number."
- "References to web-only journals should give authors, article title and journal name as above, followed by URL in full - or DOI if known - and the year of publication in parentheses."

### House forms seen in recent Nature-family reference lists (from Crossref)

- arXiv preprint: "Mitchener, L. et al. BixBench: a comprehensive benchmark for LLM-based agents in computational biology. Preprint at https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2503.00096 (2025)." (Nat. Methods 23, 1724–1733)
- bioRxiv preprint: "Li, L. et al. A systematic comparison of single-cell perturbation response prediction models. Preprint at bioRxiv https://doi.org/10.1101/2024.12.23.630036 (2024)." (Nat. Biotechnol. s41587-026-03307-w)
- NeurIPS 2025: "Wu, Y. et al. PerturBench: ... In Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 38 (NeurIPS, 2025)." (Nat. Biotechnol. s41587-026-03307-w)
- NeurIPS 2024 with pages: "Yang, J. et al. SWE-agent: ... In Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 37, 50528–50652 (NeurIPS, 2024)." (Nat. Methods 23, 749–759)
- Advance online publication with no volume yet: "Journal https://doi.org/<DOI> (year)". The AIP page notes: "All AOP articles are given a unique digital object identifier (DOI) number, which can be used to cite the paper before issue publication."

## 4. Access notes and gaps

- All five Nature Methods pages and the artwork PDF were read in full via HTTP. nature.com sends automated fetchers through an `idp.nature.com` cookie redirect, so a cookie-aware client was needed.
- The task mentions a "Nature research figure guide". The only figure guide linked from the Nature Methods AIP page is the PDF above, and that is what was used. Nature's separate web page on figure specifications was not checked.
- The Nature Methods pages do **not** state:
  - the "et al." threshold (taken from the Nature flagship guide);
  - a figure-legend word limit for Article, Analysis or Resource;
  - whether Methods references count toward the ~50-reference guideline (Nature flagship says they do not);
  - a Methods word limit (Nature flagship says the Methods "does not typically exceed 3,000 words", which is a Nature rule only).
- No Nature Methods page sets a separate Extended Data cap for Analysis or Resource. The cap of 10 applies to all article types.
