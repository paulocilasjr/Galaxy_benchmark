# Accuracy, repeatability and UDT evidence for the original layout

This material was computed from archived grades and parsed call records by `scripts/accuracy_analysis.py`. No agent execution or private answer key was opened. Primary comparisons contain four Codex configurations, 3,816 graded runs, 50 BixBench-Verified-50 tasks, 100 CompBioBench tasks and nine interpretable IWC tasks. The tenth IWC task is excluded from the graded primary comparison, as in the reviewed packages; all ten may be shown separately for operational/token observations. Astra and the superseded Claude Code configuration are not in these primary estimates.

## Suggested Results paragraphs

**Similar binary benchmark performance, with a separate IWC endpoint.** Across four configurations and three independent replicates, Galaxy-arm scores were 86.5% versus 85.2% for open-ended code on BixBench-Verified-50, a difference of 1.3 percentage points (95% paired source-capsule bootstrap interval, −3.5 to 6.4). Agreement with the reconstructed CompBioBench reference key was 87.1% versus 86.7%, a difference of 0.4 points (95% task-bootstrap interval, −1.6 to 2.4). These small estimates do not establish equivalence or noninferiority. IWC measured continuous output agreement rather than binary correctness: the Galaxy and code means were 0.980 and 0.940, respectively (difference 0.040; 95% task-bootstrap interval, 0.009 to 0.078). That difference was sensitive to failures and route definitions: excluding the three tasks containing a zero-scored run reduced it to 0.004 (−0.0004 to 0.0104). All estimates compare assigned arms, whose prompts, budgets, environments and campaigns also differed.

**Benchmark-specific configuration rankings.** The configuration with the highest observed score depended on the benchmark. GPT-5.5 led BixBench-Verified-50 in both arms (87.3% code and 89.3% Galaxy), whereas GPT-5.6 Sol led CompBioBench (91.0% and 92.0%). Sol also had the highest IWC mean in the code arm (0.988), while DeepSeek V4 Pro had the highest Galaxy-arm IWC mean (0.999). These descriptive ranks show why configuration comparisons should remain benchmark-specific; they do not prove a universal ranking or a statistically established benchmark-by-model interaction. IWC agreement is not pooled with the two binary endpoints.

**Repeatability requires an explicit outcome.** The share of task–configuration sets for which all three runs were graded correct increased from 79.5% to 82.5% in BixBench-Verified-50 and from 77.3% to 80.5% in CompBioBench. Increases occurred in all eight binary benchmark–configuration comparisons, ranging from 1 to 5 points, but every configuration-specific 95% interval included or touched zero. Discordant sets were less frequent in Galaxy when pooled within each benchmark: 15 versus 19 of 200 BixBench sets, 53 versus 66 of 400 CompBioBench sets, and 4 versus 11 of 36 IWC sets (IWC discordance: score range greater than 0.05). Only the pooled IWC discordance interval excluded equality. Discordance did not improve for every configuration: BixBench DeepSeek increased from six to seven sets, BixBench GPT-5.5 remained at one, and CompBioBench Sol increased from ten to eleven. These findings support an observed increase in all-three-correct sets, not the blanket claim that Galaxy stabilizes correct answers in every model.

**UDTs are part of the Galaxy arm.** User-defined tools allowed agents to execute their own code through Galaxy and must therefore be included in the execution-arm description. At least one UDT call was observed in 225 of 600 primary BixBench Galaxy runs and 808 of 1,200 CompBioBench runs. Request rates differed substantially by configuration: 9–67% in BixBench and 43–88% in CompBioBench. At least one UDT interface call returned `ok` in 223 of those 225 BixBench runs and 569 of the 808 CompBioBench runs. A recorded job with state `ok` occurred in 572 CompBioBench runs, a slightly larger number because fourteen calls returned `dataset_error` despite a completed job. UDT submission, job completion, returned output status and scientific correctness are separate outcomes. No UDT requests were observed on the nine primary IWC tasks. UDT use and success were selected during a run; their associations with grades cannot be interpreted as causal benefits or costs of UDTs.

## Replicate error counts

For a binary task–configuration–arm set, zero errors means three correct independent runs, one error means two correct runs, two errors means one correct run, and three errors means no correct run. These are replicate outcomes, not a trajectory of iterative correction. Split sets demonstrate run-dependent outcomes for the same task and configuration, but do not by themselves identify the cause. Three wrong runs may share an unchecked analytical choice, knowledge gap, ambiguous reference, specification problem or completion failure. Attribution requires a separate trace audit.

| Benchmark and arm | Zero errors | One error | Two errors | Three errors |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| BixBench code (200 sets) | 159 | 15 | 4 | 22 |
| BixBench Galaxy (200 sets) | 165 | 9 | 6 | 20 |
| CompBio code (400 sets) | 309 | 47 | 19 | 25 |
| CompBio Galaxy (400 sets) | 322 | 26 | 27 | 25 |

**Persistent failures recur across arms.** Comparing the code and Galaxy sets of the same task × configuration (`accuracy_cross_arm_error_transitions.csv`, `accuracy_persistent_failure_overlap.csv`): 17 BixBench pairs were rejected 3/3 in both arms (of 22 code and 20 Galaxy 3/3 sets), and 15 CompBioBench pairs (of 25 in each arm). Every BixBench task with a 3/3 Galaxy set also has one in code (7 of 7 tasks). The higher all-three-correct rate in Galaxy therefore comes from fewer mixed sets (19 versus 15; 66 versus 53), not fewer persistent failures. The census audit (`rigor_claims.md`) links persistent failures to benchmark-side causes more often than sporadic ones.

## Suggested figure structure

1. Three aligned accuracy panels, one per benchmark: four configuration points, arm means, and paired Galaxy-minus-code intervals. Use percentage points for the binary endpoints and a separate 0–1 agreement axis for IWC. A pooled estimate per benchmark is useful; a pooled estimate across benchmarks is not.
2. Configuration-specific all-three-correct differences, with confidence intervals, beside pooled 0/1/2/3 error distributions. Show IWC score-range discordance separately. Label the limited universal-direction observation explicitly rather than equating it with a proven improvement.
3. UDT use: requested/attempted runs and returned-ok runs per configuration, benchmark and denominator. A separate returned-status panel can show operational friction. Do not label requests as completed analyses or compare UDT-requesting and nonrequesting runs causally.

## Methods and export dictionary

The script uses 20,000 percentile bootstrap resamples with seed 20261002. BixBench resamples 33 shared source capsules, CompBioBench 100 tasks and IWC nine tasks. A bootstrap draw retains both arms and every replicate/configuration in a sampled cluster. Configuration intervals are descriptive and not adjusted for multiple comparisons. Newly recomputed intervals can differ slightly from older figures generated with seed 20260922. Model ranks are computed separately within each benchmark and arm using mean archived score; ties receive the minimum rank. No formal interaction test is reported.

- `accuracy_primary_runs.csv`: all primary graded observations, run identifiers and explicit eligibility flags. `score` is a binary grade for BixBench/CompBio and continuous IWC agreement. It contains no submitted answer values.
- `accuracy_benchmark_configuration.csv`: mean scores and paired-arm differences per benchmark and configuration; `scale` is 100 for percentages and 1 for IWC. Arm-specific and difference intervals are separate fields; `clusters` counts resampling units.
- `accuracy_descriptive_model_ranks.csv`: observed within-benchmark/arm rank; no rank certainty is implied.
- `accuracy_replicate_sets.csv`: three semicolon-separated grades, count correct, count incorrect, all-three-correct, discordance and within-set range/standard deviation. Binary count fields are missing for IWC rather than discretizing its agreement score.
- `accuracy_repeatability.csv`: paired-arm interval estimates for binary all-three-correct rates, benchmark-specific discordance, and continuous IWC range/standard deviation. Proportion differences are percentage points. Ratios are Galaxy/code, without percentage scaling. Undefined ratios are blank. A zero bootstrap ratio with no observed Galaxy events does not establish a true zero population rate.
- `accuracy_error_count_distribution.csv`: number/percentage of binary replicate sets containing 0–3 errors, separately for every configuration and pooled. Pooled rows duplicate the corresponding underlying observations for display and must not be summed with configuration rows.
- `accuracy_sensitivities.csv`: retrospective exclusions of benchmark-side BixBench cases, outcome-named CompBio cells, IWC zero-score tasks, ATAC-seq, and unmatched IWC budgets. These are sensitivities, not a prospective unbiased correction or a causal reanalysis.
- `accuracy_udt_call_observations.csv`: each primary UDT interface call, its returned/client status, submission metadata, returned job count/states, and independently derived submission/job/returned-ok flags. A job record does not establish execution; a recorded `ok` job establishes reported completion, not scientific correctness.
- `accuracy_udt_run_observations.csv`: graded Galaxy runs plus call counts and run-level flags. Missing trace is explicit. The operational trajectory categories describe the strongest observed evidence; “no request observed” is not a proof of no attempt in a missing trace.
- `accuracy_udt_usage.csv`: denominators and attempted/submitted/job-record/job-ok/returned-ok counts. UDT job-ok and interface returned-ok counts differ where output validation failed after job completion.
- `accuracy_udt_accuracy_association.csv`: mean grade within operational trajectory categories; these are unadjusted descriptive associations. IWC `correct_runs` is missing because the endpoint remains continuous.
- `accuracy_answer_vote_sensitivity.csv`: outcome-blind normalized-answer voting records from the shared reviewed implementation. The earliest submitted representative in the majority identity group supplies the archived grade. Identity is defined retrospectively at ten or three significant digits, retains percent units, and is distinct from biological correctness or exact answer text.

`accuracy_results.json` collects the estimates, interpretation constraints and input SHA-256 hashes. The upstream grading provenance and CompBioBench key uncertainty remain those described in the existing reviewed package. These exports do not independently adjudicate scientific correctness or validate every benchmark reference.
