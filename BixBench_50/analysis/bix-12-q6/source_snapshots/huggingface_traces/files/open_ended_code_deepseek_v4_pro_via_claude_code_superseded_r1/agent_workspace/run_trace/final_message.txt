The answer `6748.0` is confirmed. Here's a summary of the analysis:

**Data**: 241 animal single-copy ortholog MSAs (4 species: *C. elegans*, *G. gallus*, *M. musculus*, *X. laevis*) and 255 fungal MSAs (4 species: *N. crassa*, *S. cerevisiae*, *S. pombe*, *U. maydis*), all from `scogs_animals.zip` and `scogs_fungi.zip`.

**Method**: For each MSA, counted parsimony-informative sites — alignment columns with ≥2 standard amino acids, each present in ≥2 sequences (gaps excluded as missing data). Then computed the Mann-Whitney U statistic comparing the distribution of these raw counts between animals (group 1) and fungi (group 2).

**Results**:
- Animal PIS: mean 1.11, median 0, range [0, 29]
- Fungal PIS: mean 21.85, median 19, range [0, 162]
- **Mann-Whitney U (animals) = 6748.0** (expected under H₀: 30727.5)

The substantially lower U for animals reflects fewer parsimony-informative sites in animal ortholog alignments, consistent with slower evolutionary rates in animals compared to fungi.
